Learn to read music on the guitar

A note appears on the staff. You play it. Openstring listens and tells you whether you found it, how long you took, and what to work on next.

Nothing you play leaves this device. The audio is analysed in your browser and never recorded or uploaded — no recording is ever made. There is no subscription, ever. Your progress is saved in this browser; if you want it on your phone too you can sign in with an email link, and even then only the numbers travel, never the sound.

Never read music before? That is fine — start anyway. The first stage is eight notes in one octave, and it shows you where each one lives whenever you ask.

This browser cannot record audio, so the listening part will not work. Try Chrome, Edge, Safari or Firefox — and note the page must be served over https.

Play the rhythm against the click

This is the half most free tools skip. Pitch does not matter here — play any note, or tap the body of the guitar. What is measured is when.

One bar of count-in, then play.
About timing accuracy

Your computer adds a small, unknown delay between the string moving and the browser hearing it. That means consistency — whether your notes are evenly spaced — is always measured correctly, but rushing or dragging is only as accurate as the delay estimate below. Headphones are strongly recommended: through speakers the microphone hears the click itself and counts it as a note.

Practise a piece

Bring in a piece as MusicXML — Guitar Pro and MuseScore both export it (File → Export → MusicXML, uncompressed .musicxml or .xml). Openstring splits it into short chunks, drills each one, then drills the joins between them, raising the tempo only when you play it clean.
